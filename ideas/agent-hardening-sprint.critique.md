## What the revision changed

## Round 2

### market-skeptic — 5/10, conditional

The revision is honest: it now targets only firms with a written trigger and needs about 24 sprints a year, which is a low bar. But no evidence yet shows that the trigger exists. AI questionnaires ask about data handling and shadow AI, not red-teaming coding agents. The problem evidence is still developer GitHub issues. Demand is plausible but untested.

Strongest point: The demand bar is now realistic: about 24 paying engagements a year, and a two-week, deposit-based test that asks partners and CTOs for written triggers before any build.

- **major**: The whole concept now rests on the trigger, and no evidence shows that it exists. The buyer is a CTO with a written customer-questionnaire item, auditor question or near-miss about coding agents. Published AI vendor questionnaire guidance (Vanta, GRC templates) asks about training on customer data, prompt retention, sub-processors and shadow AI. It does not ask for adversarial testing of developers' agents, MCP permissions or CI agent jobs. The concept cites no questionnaire, auditor or customer that asks for this. SOC 2 has no AI criteria (CSA). Without such a document, a red team is a discretionary $6,500. ([evidence](https://www.vanta.com/resources/questions-to-ask-ai-vendors; concept:riskiest_assumption; concept:risks[0]))
- **major**: The problem evidence still comes from developers and tool authors. The new items (headless MCP auto-approval, an MCP read-only bypass, a missing subagent validator) are bugs in agent and MCP projects, reported by their own maintainers or users. They show that the risk is technically real. They do not show that a small-company CTO will pay an outsider to find it. None of them involves a target-profile company that was harmed, or one that asked for help. — _concept:problem.evidence; input:themes[0].need_statements_
- **minor**: The budget for AI-coding security goes to a different pain. Buyers worry most about defects in generated code (Black Duck: 64%), and SAST/SCA and code-review vendors serve that. The concept chose not to change this and treats it as out of scope. That is defensible, but it means the sprint competes for leftover discretionary budget at firms without an AppSec engineer. ([evidence](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ai-coding-adoption-governance-lags/))
- **minor**: Urgency still depends on an incident. Wiz's GhostApproval and other coding-assistant vulnerability disclosures raise awareness. A widely reported breach of a small SaaS company through an injected coding agent would turn deferral into purchases, and the founder cannot make that happen. — _concept:risks[0]_
- **minor**: Partner resale assumes that pentest and vCISO firms see client pull for this line item. If they don't, they will not push a $5,000 wholesale SKU. If they do, they can build the service in-house from the same free native controls. — _concept:go_to_market.channel_

Round-1 objections:
- ❌ not resolved: The demand signal comes from the wrong people (developer GitHub issues, vendor-filed #60246) — #60246 and the vendor-filed items were removed, which fixes the bias. The replacement evidence is still developer bug reports, though. Buyer-side demand is acknowledged and goes to the riskiest-assumption test, but it is still not shown.
- ❌ not resolved: The must-have trigger (SOC 2 or auditor demand) is assumed — This is now stated honestly. The buyer is narrowed to firms with a written trigger, and the test targets that trigger. The trigger itself is still unproven, and published AI questionnaires don't ask about this.
- ❌ not resolved: Buyers' budgeted pain is insecure generated code, not agent tool-call hardening — Deliberately unchanged. The concept targets a narrower segment instead. This is accepted as a positioning choice and downgraded to minor.
- ❌ not resolved: Urgency depends on an incident — This is still in risks. It is outside the founder's control.
- ✅ resolved: The reachable slice is narrow — For a solo services firm that needs about 24 sprints a year, low thousands of candidate firms is enough, if the trigger exists.

Would change my mind: 3 of 10 SOC 2 readiness, vCISO or pentest firms show redacted client questionnaires or audit requests that ask about coding-agent, MCP or CI-agent controls; At least 1 CTO deposit of $1,000 toward a $6,500 sprint, or 2 paid one-repository CI tests, within the 2-week test; A major questionnaire standard (SIG, CAIQ) or a large enterprise buyer's vendor-security template adds explicit coding-agent or MCP tool-permission questions

Promptfoo gives away coding-agent injection plugins, and Appsecco already sells fixed-price agent-workflow tests with retest. That leaves roughly a 6-12 month window and a thin moat.

Strongest point: Repositioned as testing that complements Vanta, Drata and Kirin rather than competing with them. Before Spring 2027, no platform attacks a small company's real coding-agent, MCP and CI setup.

- **major**: Most dangerous competitor is still Vanta, and the threat now hits the core. - **minor**: The partner resellers (pentest, vCISO and SOC 2 readiness firms) are also the likeliest direct competitors. Channel coverage of the Vanta-Pillar deal already tells MSPs and compliance partners to build their own adversarial-testing and AI-governance services. With free Promptfoo plugins and a published OWASP agentic list, a $5,000 wholesale sprint competes with partners doing it themselves. ([evidence](https://www.channelinsider.com/channel-business/mergers-and-acquisitions/news-vanta-pillar-security-deal-ai-protection/))

### security-practitioner — 7/10, conditional

The revision fixes the core round-1 problems. Controls are now delivered centrally and fail closed, CI fixes rest on least privilege, and testing uses a disposable environment with an honest bypass list. A practitioner would accept that. The remaining gap: the native sandbox does not contain MCP servers, the exposure the concept sells against. Disposable-environment findings and buyers without central delivery also weaken the risk reduction.

Strongest point: Hands-on injection tests plus deterministic CI fixes (least-privilege GITHUB_TOKEN, no secrets on untrusted triggers, gated writes) cut real risk and come with an honest stated-bypass list.

- **major**: The hardening leans on the native sandbox with an egress allowlist, but Claude Code's sandbox covers shell commands only. File tools, MCP servers and hooks run outside it. The concept's headline exposure is MCP servers wired to GitHub, cloud and production data, so a poisoned issue that makes the agent call a write-capable MCP tool is not contained by the sandbox. MCP risk has to be cut at the server: an allowlist (allowedMcpServers/managed-mcp.json), per-tool deny rules, least-privilege tokens for each MCP server, and read-only database roles. None of these is named as a deliverable. ([evidence](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sandboxing (sandbox covers shell commands only; MCP servers and hooks run outside it); concept:mvp.features[3]; concept:problem.evidence))
- **minor**: Central enforcement is uneven across the four agents. Claude Code has server-managed settings on Teams/Enterprise and MDM delivery. Cursor's team and enterprise-managed hooks are Enterprise-only, and Cursor cloud agents skip beforeMCPExecution hooks and run no hooks during read-only early turns. The client gets one hardening outcome per agent, and a 15-150 person buyer on Cursor Pro or Copilot Business may end up with advisory controls. The concept admits this ('best fit', 'told their guardrails are advisory'), but it should be checked before the client pays. ([evidence](https://cursor.com/docs/agent/hooks (Enterprise team hooks; MCP hooks not available in cloud agents); concept:buyer.company_profile))
- **minor**: Default testing runs on a fork with scoped test tokens and possibly mock MCP servers. That makes access easy to grant, but the findings show whether the configuration can be injected, not what the real tokens can reach. A CTO may read 'injection succeeded on a mock' as low real risk. The report needs a blast-radius section built from the real token scopes, which the client enumerates. — _concept:mvp.features[1]_
- **minor**: Hooks set to fail closed on every tool call will break developer workflows whenever an agent update changes tool names or input formats. Teams under deadline then add excludedCommands or loosen rules, so the controls drift between quarterly re-tests. The re-test catches drift late. A config-drift check, such as /status showing managed sources or a CI lint of settings, would catch it sooner. — _concept:mvp.features[5]; concept:build.hardest_parts[2]_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: The 'enforced tool policy' is client-side and bypassable without Teams/Enterprise or MDM. — Delivery moved to server-managed settings/MDM with allowManagedHooksOnly; 'enforced' wording dropped; buyers without central delivery are told their controls are advisory. Coverage across agents is still uneven (new minor).
- ✅ resolved: Hooks fail open, a custom shell-AST parser will break, and parsing misses script/interpreter indirection; native sandboxing missing. — Custom parser dropped for maintained OSS guards, hooks fail closed, native sandbox with egress allowlist added, and indirection cases added to the suite. The sandbox does not cover MCP servers (new major).
- ✅ resolved: The red-team and inventory access request is hard to grant (secrets in MCP configs, real production agents). — Client-run inventory emits a reviewed summary only; testing defaults to a disposable fork with scoped tokens. The trade-off is lower realism (new minor).
- ✅ resolved: CI canary catches only verbatim exfiltration; least privilege is the real fix. — Canary demoted to a tripwire; least-privilege GITHUB_TOKEN, no secrets on untrusted triggers and gated writes are now primary, using zizmor and the gh-aw pattern.
- ✅ resolved: Local hook logs are tamper-prone and weak SOC 2 evidence. — Logs are shipped to the client's central store where one exists, and the concept no longer claims tamper-resistant evidence otherwise.

Would change my mind: MCP-specific hardening added as a deliverable: allowlist plus per-server least-privilege tokens or read-only roles, and a re-test showing a poisoned issue can no longer trigger a write-capable MCP tool.; Inventory data from 10 prospects showing at least half have Claude Teams/Enterprise, Cursor Enterprise or MDM, so central delivery is the common case.; One pilot report where a blast-radius section built from the client's real token scopes changed what the client fixed.

### technical-feasibility — 7/10, go

The revision fixed the main build risks: the custom compiler and parser are gone, central managed settings and an OS sandbox are used, and the test environment and suite are budgeted in 3 months. Gaps remain. The sandbox covers shell commands only, not MCP servers, the main injection path. Sandbox and central delivery are uneven across the four agents, and one person must keep the coverage matrix current.

Strongest point: The build is now mostly configuration and testing of documented vendor controls (managed settings, OS sandbox, fail-closed hooks, zizmor, gh-aw), which one skilled tester can deliver and maintain.

- **major**: The new containment layer does not cover the main attack surface. Claude Code's sandbox covers shell commands only. Local MCP servers, hooks and the built-in file and web tools run outside it with full user access, and allowedDomains does not limit WebFetch. A poisoned issue that drives an MCP tool such as GitHub, a database or mail is limited only by the MCP allowlist, permission rules and token scope, not by the sandbox or the egress allowlist. The hardening must add per-server least-privilege tokens, deny rules on MCP write tools and possibly containerised MCP servers. The bypass list must say plainly that the sandbox does not cover MCP. ([evidence](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sandboxing.md ('The sandbox covers shell commands only. Claude's file tools, MCP servers, and hooks run outside it'; local MCP servers 'run with your full access'); concept:mvp.features[3]))
- **minor**: The sandbox fails open unless configured otherwise. By default, when the sandbox is unsupported or its dependencies are missing, commands run unsandboxed. This covers native Windows, Linux machines without bubblewrap or socat, and containers where bwrap cannot start. An allow rule also approves an unsandboxed retry. The kit must set failIfUnavailable, allowUnsandboxedCommands: false and allowManagedDomainsOnly in managed settings. A 25-developer rollout will also need allowlist tuning when builds break. A 2-week sprint must budget for that tuning or ship the sandbox in audit mode. ([evidence](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sandboxing.md (failIfUnavailable; unsandboxed-retry escape hatch; allowUnsandboxedCommands)))
- **minor**: Central delivery and sandbox parity still differ by agent. Claude Code server-managed settings require Teams or Enterprise. Cursor team hooks are Enterprise only, and Cursor's sandbox has its own platform and network modes. Copilot cloud agent supports only a subset of hook events, and Gemini uses a separate TOML policy engine. Dropping the compiler removes the code to maintain, but one founder still has to keep the per-agent coverage matrix and the 30+ injection-case suite re-validated across weekly releases. For clients on mixed tools, part of the hardening will stay advisory, as the concept now admits. ([evidence](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/server-managed-settings.md (Teams/Enterprise only); https://cursor.com/docs/agent/terminal; concept:build.hardest_parts[2]))
- **minor**: Three months is tight for everything now listed: a disposable-environment method, a versioned 30+ case suite with interpreter indirection, a self-scan inventory tool, engagement paperwork with insurance, and 2-3 public write-ups. Vendor-acknowledged findings depend on disclosure timelines the founder does not control. The build itself is feasible, but the write-ups may push the first sale past month 3. — _concept:build.months_to_mvp; concept:build.hardest_parts[0]_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: The shell-AST differentiator is largely native and not a security boundary; interpreter indirection needs an OS sandbox and egress limits. — The custom parser was dropped. A native sandbox with an egress allowlist and indirection test cases were added. The sandbox's MCP and fail-open gaps remain as new objections.
- ✅ resolved: Controls are not enforced without central delivery; hooks fail open and project/user hooks are editable. — Delivery now runs through server-managed settings or MDM with allowManagedHooksOnly and fail-closed hooks. Clients without central delivery are told their guardrails are advisory, and the 'enforced' wording was removed.
- ✅ resolved: One YAML policy compiled into four agents is a maintenance burden with silent holes. — The compiler was dropped in favour of native controls, a coverage matrix and stated gaps. Upkeep of the matrix and suite remains, now minor.
- ✅ resolved: The CI canary catches only verbatim leaks and is no substitute for removing write tokens and secrets. — The canary is now only a tripwire. A least-privilege GITHUB_TOKEN, no secrets on untrusted triggers, gh-aw read-only jobs and zizmor are the primary fix.
- ✅ resolved: A disposable red-team environment was not budgeted in the one-month plan or the $3,500 sprint. — The disposable fork, scoped tokens and mock MCP servers are now an MVP feature and a hardest part. The MVP takes 3 months, and the price is $6,500.

Would change my mind: A pilot client where an MCP-driven injection case (poisoned issue to a GitHub or database MCP write) is blocked by token scope or deny rules, not only shell cases by the sandbox.; A managed-settings template with failIfUnavailable, allowUnsandboxedCommands: false and allowManagedDomainsOnly, rolled out to 20+ developers with under a day of allowlist tuning.; A coverage matrix for the four agents, kept current through two release cycles in under one day a month.

### technical-feasibility — 6/10, conditional

A one-month MVP is realistic for a services kit: inventory script, workflow scanner, manual red team, hook configs. Two claims are weaker than stated. 'Enforced' controls rest on hooks that fail open, can be overridden unless pushed by MDM or enterprise plans, and miss interpreter indirection. Strongest point: The building blocks are real and documented. Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot and Gemini all expose blocking pre-tool hooks or policy engines, so a skilled solo tester can deliver working controls without building a platform.
