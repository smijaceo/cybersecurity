## What the revision changed

- [competitor-analyst] Scimify is a self-serve, OIN-listed SCIM bridge from $0.90/user/month with unlimited apps, undercutting per-app pricing several times over → dropped per-app pricing and the bridge; repriced at $1/user/month unlimited apps; positioned as complementary to Scimify.
- [competitor-analyst] Browser-agent long tail is contested by Cerby, Aquera, Stitchflow and Zluri → removed browser agents entirely; long tail covered by CSV/export ingestion; added Cerby to competitors.
- [competitor-analyst] Orphan-account verification is a thin moat (Aquera audit reports, SaaS-management reconciliation) → not fully changed: verification is now the whole product, differentiated by being read-only and path-agnostic; kept as the top bundling risk.

### competitor-analyst — 4/10, no-go

The pivot to read-only verification gets out of Aquera's and Scimify's way, but walks into the buyer's compliance platform. Vanta and Drata already start leaver checks from Okta or Entra deactivation, check integrated apps and fail tests on leftover accounts. Nudge Security sells offboarding beyond SSO. What remains unique is CSV ingestion and chase-up, a thin feature-sized moat.

Strongest point: Independent, path-agnostic, read-only verification is a cleaner trust story than any write-side bridge. Long-tail apps without integrations are a real blind spot in Vanta and Drata offboarding.

- **fatal**: Most dangerous competitor: Vanta, with Drata close behind. The buyer is defined by a SOC 2 or ISO 27001 cycle, so most already pay for one of them. Vanta starts offboarding when the IdP suspends a user (or the HRIS marks them terminated). It lists the leaver's known accounts, auto-detects deactivation in monitored integrations, assigns tasks for the rest, and blocks completion until those are done. Drata's tests fail when a terminated person keeps access in connected systems, under a one-business-day control. LeaverCheck's per-leaver check, chase-up and auditor log largely duplicate a tool the buyer already owns, and the concept's competitor table omits both. ([evidence](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/11345609-offboarding-personnel ; https://help.drata.com/en/articles/4777040-test-infrastructure-accounts-properly-removed ; concept:buyer.company_profile ; concept:competitors))
- **major**: The remaining wedge, apps with no integration, is a short step for the incumbents. Vanta already has a private-integration API that pushes user accounts in and links them to IdP users for account tests, an Access File Uploader for apps it can't connect to, and an offboarding API. Adding 'upload this app's export, diff it against leavers' to offboarding is a feature, not a product, and could ship next quarter. Meanwhile the GTM plans to partner with these same compliance-automation tools, which hands the most likely acquirer-or-cloner the roadmap. ([evidence](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/11345421-access-file-uploader ; https://developer.vanta.com/docs/offboard-people ; concept:go_to_market.channel))
- **major**: Nudge Security sells offboarding for exactly the 'beyond the IdP' gap, and is missing from the table. It discovers SaaS accounts, shadow IT and OAuth grants per identity, including apps outside SSO. It nudges IT or users to remove access where it can't automate. It uses password resets to lock leavers out of unmanaged accounts. It offers 'find lingering access on Day One' of a free trial, the same hook as LeaverCheck's free scan, but needs no CSVs from app owners. ([evidence](https://nudgesecurity.com/use-cases/it-offboarding))
- **major**: SaaS-management and IGA platforms (Zluri, Torii, Lumos, Stitchflow, ConductorOne) already sell user-list reconciliation, and Okta ISPM covers identity posture. ConductorOne's Apache-2.0 Baton toolkit lists principals across apps and diffs sync runs, now continued in baton-sdk. That gives a scripting IT team, or any bridge vendor such as Scimify or Aquera, a free read-only connector base to build the same check. ([evidence](https://github.com/ConductorOne/baton ; concept:risks))
- **minor**: Price positioning is awkward. At $1/user/month LeaverCheck costs more than Scimify, which starts at $0.90 and actually deprovisions. A buyer could fairly ask why the checker costs more than the doer, and the bridge vendor can bundle a verification report at near-zero marginal cost. — _concept:pricing.entry_price_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: Aquera already ships the exact hosted SCIM gateway architecture with both IdP channels. — The concept dropped the write-side gateway and lists Aquera as a path it verifies, not a rival product.
- ✅ resolved: Scimify is the self-serve, OIN-listed bridge at $0.90/user/month with unlimited apps, which inverts per-app pricing. — The concept no longer sells a bridge and dropped per-app pricing. But $1/user/month for checking, above Scimify's price for doing the deprovisioning, is a new and smaller pricing tension (minor).
- ✅ resolved: The browser-agent long tail is contested by Cerby, Aquera, Stitchflow and Zluri. — Browser agents are removed. The long tail is covered by CSV exports, so it no longer competes on automation depth.
- ❌ not resolved: Nightly orphan-account verification is a thin moat (Aquera audit reports, SaaS-management reconciliation, Baton). — Verification is now the whole product, so this objection got worse. Vanta and Drata offboarding checks, Nudge Security and open-source Baton all cover the same ground for the target buyer.
- ✅ resolved: A late entrant competes for OIN shelf space against established Okta partners. — The product no longer seeks the same OIN slot. Bundling risk has moved from Okta partners to the compliance platforms the buyer already pays for.

Would change my mind: At least 6 of the 20 concierge scans find leftover accounts at companies that already run Vanta or Drata offboarding, in apps those platforms mark as unmonitored or don't list.; A signed integration or marketplace partnership with Vanta or Drata, where LeaverCheck pushes verified account status into their offboarding through the private-integration API, priced as an add-on they won't build themselves.; Evidence that Nudge Security and SaaS-management tools miss at least 30% of the non-SCIM leftover accounts LeaverCheck finds in a head-to-head on the same customers.; At least 5 paying customers who already own Vanta, Drata or Nudge and still buy LeaverCheck at $1/user/month or more.

### security-practitioner — 7/10, conditional

The pivot to read-only verification fixes the trust problem. IT teams can approve read tokens and exports, and the product can't break anything. What's left is assurance quality. Trigger delivery is best-effort, export-fed apps are self-reported and stale, and only named user records are checked. Any of these can turn a 'checks run' log into false comfort unless the gaps are shown explicitly.

Strongest point: Read-only and independent of the write path: it verifies whatever offboarding path the buyer uses, can't make a destructive mistake, and fits a light security review.

- **major**: The trigger isn't reliable. Okta event hooks are best-effort, retry at most once, time out after 3 seconds and guarantee no maximum delay, so a dropped deactivation event means that leaver is never checked, with no visible error. Leavers who are suspended, unassigned from the app group, or disabled by HR-driven Entra sync (rather than deactivated) may not fire the expected event at all. Without a periodic reconciliation against the System Log, Entra audit logs and directory state, the per-leaver log misses exactly the cases it exists to catch. ([evidence](https://developer.okta.com/docs/concepts/event-hooks/; concept:mvp.features[0]))
- **major**: Export-fed apps get weak assurance. In the long tail, which is also where SSO usually isn't enforced and the real risk sits, the data the check relies on comes from the same app owner who is supposed to remove the account. That owner can upload a stale, filtered or edited CSV, and nothing proves the export is complete or recent. A 'checked' entry built on a three-week-old export looks the same in an audit log as a live API read unless the log records the data source, the export's timestamp and who uploaded it, and flags stale exports as not checked. — _concept:mvp.features[2]; concept:mvp.features[5]; concept:risks[2]_
- **major**: Risk coverage is incomplete. A leaver is only truly gone when their personal API tokens, OAuth grants to third-party apps, active sessions, shared or service accounts they knew the password to, and SSH or deploy keys are also gone. A user-list check sees none of these. In SSO-enforced apps, which are the API-connected ones, the main residual risk is exactly these tokens, so a 'no leftover account' result can still leave live access. The pitch that leavers are 'actually gone everywhere' overstates what the product checks. — _concept:buyer.company_profile; concept:mvp.features[1]_
- **minor**: Truly read-only scopes are uneven. Several common apps need an admin or 'view setup' role to list all users with their status, and the concept already says some apps only offer admin tokens. If, in practice, a quarter or more of the 12 connectors need admin-equivalent tokens, the multi-tenant credential-concentration problem returns in a smaller form, and security reviews get longer again. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[1]_
- **minor**: The data store is a reconnaissance prize. Each tenant's database holds the full user lists of many apps, plus emails and the leaver timeline: a map of who has access where, and which leftover accounts are still live. That is attractive to phishers and insiders. Customers will ask for tenant isolation, encryption, short retention and a way to keep findings from becoming an attack list. — _concept:risks[4]_
- **minor**: Alert fatigue risk. Contractors on personal domains, shared mailboxes, renamed users and app owners who never act will create a stream of reopened tasks. If the false-positive rate or the share of overdue tasks is high, IT will mute the Slack channel and the closed loop will stop working. — _concept:mvp.features[3]; concept:mvp.features[4]_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: Unattended browser connectors need SSO-bypass admin accounts and TOTP seeds. — Browser agents, stored passwords and TOTP seeds are out of scope; the long tail is covered by exports.
- ✅ resolved: Concentration risk: a pre-SOC 2 startup holds admin credentials for many apps across many customers. — Largely resolved: only read tokens are held and admin-only tokens are refused. A smaller version of the risk remains where list-users needs an admin role, plus aggregated directory data.
- ✅ resolved: Async SCIM gives false assurance in the IdP audit log. — The product is out of the SCIM path, but a new assurance gap appears: event hooks are best-effort, so checks can be silently missed without reconciliation.
- ✅ resolved: Wrong-account deprovisioning from fragile identity matching. — The product never writes, and ambiguous matches are flagged for review. The remaining cost of bad matching is alert noise, not damage.
- ❌ not resolved: Risk reduction is uneven where SSO is already enforced. — Partly addressed by prioritising non-SSO apps, but those are mostly export-fed with weak assurance, and token or OAuth residual risk in SSO apps isn't checked.
- ✅ resolved: Cloud-only browser agent excludes buyers who require on-prem admin sessions. — Moot: there's no browser agent, and customers upload exports themselves.

Would change my mind: A daily reconciliation against the Okta System Log, Entra audit logs and directory state, which catches 100% of leavers in a pilot, including ones whose event hook delivery failed.; Every check-log entry carries its data source, data timestamp and uploader, and stale exports are marked unchecked; auditors at 2 or more firms accept the format.; 10 or more of the 12 launch connectors work with a genuinely read-only scope on the plan tiers buyers actually hold.; Phase-2 checks for API tokens and OAuth grants in at least the top 5 apps, or the marketing explicitly scoped to 'user accounts only'.; A pilot with 5 or more customers where at least 80% of findings are confirmed real, and app owners close at least 70% of tasks before the deadline.

### technical-feasibility — 7/10, go

The read-only pivot makes this buildable. The work is an event listener, about 12 list-user connectors, CSV ingestion, matching and a task loop, which two engineers can ship in 4 months. What's left is reliability work: Okta hooks are best-effort, read scopes leave blind spots, and export-fed apps can't meet the one-hour promise. All of it is fixable.

Strongest point: Reading user lists and diffing them against deactivations is a small, stable integration surface with no write path, browser agents or MFA. The scope now fits two engineers in four months.

- **major**: The trigger can't be relied on by itself. Okta event hooks are best-effort: delivery is at least once, Okta retries at most once, it doesn't retry 4xx responses, it times out after 3 seconds and it guarantees no maximum delay. A dropped hook means a leaver who is never checked, which gives silent false assurance. Entra has no equivalent push for audit events, so its checks depend on polling the audit log, and Entra Free keeps that log for only 7 days. The MVP needs a System Log and audit-log poller that reconciles against the hooks, idempotent handling of event IDs, and alerts when a poll falls behind. ([evidence](https://developer.okta.com/docs/concepts/event-hooks/ (best-effort, at most one retry, no max delay); https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/monitoring-health/reference-reports-data-retention (audit logs 7 days on Free); concept:mvp.features[0]))
- **major**: Read-only list endpoints don't see everything, and some only come with admin-grade tokens. Notion's list-users excludes guests, so guest and contractor leftovers are invisible to it, and personal access tokens can't list users at all. Atlassian org API keys last at most a year, and without scopes they can reach every admin API. Slack needs users:read plus users:read.email to match on email. Each connector needs a documented coverage boundary (which account types, which plan tiers) shown per leaver, or the check log overstates what was checked. ([evidence](https://developers.notion.com/reference/get-users (guests not included; PATs cannot list users); https://support.atlassian.com/organization-administration/docs/manage-an-organization-with-the-admin-apis/ (key max one year; unscoped keys reach all admin APIs); https://docs.slack.dev/reference/methods/users.list; concept:mvp.features[1]))
- **minor**: The one-liner promises a check of every app within an hour, but export-fed apps can only be checked as fresh as their last upload, which could be weeks old. The product must label each result with the age of its source data and not report an app as verified from a stale export. Otherwise the core promise is technically false for the long tail. — _concept:one_liner; concept:mvp.features[2]; concept:build.hardest_parts[2]_
- **minor**: There's a timing race with the write path. Bridges, Okta LCM and Entra's periodic provisioning cycle deprovision asynchronously, so a check that runs right after deactivation will flag accounts that are still being removed. Checks need per-path grace windows and scheduled re-checks before anything is escalated to an app owner, or the first week of alerts will be mostly noise. — _concept:mvp.features[4]_
- **minor**: Matching on immutable IdP IDs only works where an app stores the SSO subject. Non-SSO apps, which are the main target, mostly expose only an email and a display name. Expect a large manual-review queue at onboarding, and a design for persistent manual links that survive email renames. — _concept:mvp.features[3]; concept:build.hardest_parts[0]_
- **minor**: OAuth app registration and marketplace review (Slack app distribution, Atlassian, others) for about 12 vendors, along with token rotation, lands on two engineers. SOC 2 Type II can't be finished by month 4 because it needs an observation period. The plan should target Type I or readiness at launch. — _concept:build.months_to_mvp; concept:build.hardest_parts[3]_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: Paywalled apps often paywall user-management APIs too (Notion is list-only), which pushes coverage onto browser agents — The read-only design only needs list-users, which Notion and others expose, and apps without it go through exports. The remaining gap is visibility (Notion omits guests), not a forced browser path.
- ✅ resolved: The scope doesn't fit 2 engineers in 5 months — The SCIM server, browser platform and write vault are gone. About 12 read connectors, CSV ingestion, matching and a task loop is a credible 4-month MVP for two engineers.
- ✅ resolved: The browser path needs an IdP service identity with a stored MFA factor or a break-glass admin — Browser agents and stored admin credentials are out of scope.
- ✅ resolved: Connector maintenance cost exceeds the per-app price — Read-only list endpoints change rarely, and per-user pricing pools upkeep across apps. Token rotation (Atlassian keys last at most a year) is still a chore but a small one.
- ✅ resolved: SCIM client quirks differ between Okta and Entra — There's no SCIM server. It's replaced by a dependency on hook reliability and audit-log polling, which is raised as a new objection.
- ✅ resolved: OIN and Entra gallery listing is per-app — There's one IdP-side integration. Per-vendor OAuth app registration replaces it as a smaller task.

Would change my mind: A per-app matrix for the 12 target connectors showing a non-admin list-users scope on the tiers buyers actually hold, plus which account types (guests, contractors, bots) each one misses; A prototype that uses Okta hooks plus System Log reconciliation and loses no deactivations over 30 days in a test org, with measured detection latency; Data from the concierge scans showing a false-positive rate under 10% after grace windows, and the share of matches that needed manual review; Measured connector upkeep of 2 engineer-hours or less per connector per month over the pilot

### gtm-pricing — 4/10, no-go

The read-only pivot fixes the trust problem and makes pricing coherent, but it walks into the SOC 2 buyer's existing compliance platform. Vanta-class tools already check offboarding through read-only integrations, and the concept courts them as partners. What remains is a $3-6K ACV for unproven pain, sold founder-led with concierge scans, plus churn after each audit.

Strongest point: The free orphan-account scan is a crisp, low-friction wedge with a tangible result, and read-only scope genuinely shortens security review at a card-swipe price.

- **major**: The target buyer (a SOC 2/ISO shop with an audit in the next six months) very likely already pays for a compliance-automation platform. Vanta's offboarding workflow reportedly detects accounts in connected apps through mostly read-only integrations and auto-closes the task once deprovisioning is detected. Apps without an integration sit under 'Unmonitored Accounts', where an admin attests removal by hand. That covers most of LeaverCheck's value for API apps inside a budget line the buyer already has, so the channel plan to 'partner with compliance-automation tools' means courting the most dangerous substitute. What's left is better coverage of unmonitored apps, and that relies on CSV uploads, which is not much better than the attestation the buyer already does. ([evidence](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/11345609-offboarding-personnel (from search results; the page itself could not be opened from this session); concept:go_to_market.channel; concept:buyer.company_profile))
- **major**: The price is set by a competitor anchor, not by value, and the anchor works against it. The rationale benchmarks Scimify at $0.90/user/month, but Scimify does the deprovisioning. Charging $1/user/month just to check is more than the bridge that does the work, which is a hard sentence for a buyer to accept. Per-IdP-user pricing also charges for every employee when the value sits in a handful of leavers a month on 10+ non-SCIM apps. — _concept:pricing.rationale; concept:pricing.entry_price; concept:competitors_
- **major**: The unit economics are still thin. At $250/month minimum and about $6K for 500 users, a typical deal is $3-6K a year. ### legal-ethics — 7/10, go

- **minor**: A read-only list-users token is still a full directory of the customer's staff, guests and contractors in each app. Many of them are an admin-scoped token with a read label, and the concept says it will 'confine' some admin tokens rather than refuse them outright.
