## Round 2

### market-skeptic — 4/10, conditional

The revision is honest and the cost pitch is gone, but the demand evidence did not change: old in-house admin questions, with no MSSP owner asking to buy. Narrowing to Wazuh-running MSSPs shrinks a market that was already small. Whether clients want the compliance-driven evidence report is untested. It stays a hypothesis until the 15-interview and paid-pilot test passes.

Strongest point: The test is now aimed at the actual buyer. It runs 15 calls with Wazuh-running MSSPs, asks for payment up front and has a clear pass bar, so it will cheaply show whether demand exists.

- **major**: There is still no buyer-side demand signal. All eight problem.evidence items come from in-house admins, a vendor blog or an enterprise survey, and most date from 2009-2021. The concept's own risks admit this. The new value claims (per-tenant drift alerts, a resellable client evidence report) have no evidence at all that any MSSP or SMB client asked for them. — _concept:problem.evidence; concept:risks[2]; https://serverfault.com/questions/13451/terminal-server-logging_
- **major**: Narrowing the target makes the market smaller. The target is now MSSPs with 2-15 analysts that run their own Wazuh for Windows-heavy SMB clients and will not use Huntress, Blumira or LimaCharlie. That is a subset of a niche already estimated at low thousands. At $9 per tenant ($450/month at 50 tenants, about $5.4k/year), even 200 customers is roughly $1M ARR. The concept still gives no count. Its only route to scale is a Wazuh or RMM partner, and that is an open question. — _concept:buyer.company_profile; concept:pricing.entry_price; concept:open_questions[0]; concept:open_questions[2]_
- **major**: The forcing function is assumed, not shown. The pitch now depends on PCI DSS 10, HIPAA and cyber-insurance clients asking their MSSP for server logon evidence. The concept lists whether insurers or auditors actually ask for this as an open question. If they don't, the client report is a nice-to-have, and the MSSP cannot easily resell it per client. — _concept:open_questions[1]; concept:go_to_market.first_customers_
- **minor**: Recurring demand is doubtful. Once packs are deployed, drift is occasional. The concept makes month-3 renewal an open question, which means the recurring part of the revenue is unproven. A one-off $2,500 onboarding may be all that many MSSPs pay for. — _concept:open_questions[3]_
- **minor**: The claim that free Wazuh has no scheduled per-client report needs checking. The Wazuh dashboard is built on OpenSearch Dashboards, whose Reporting feature makes recurring and cron-scheduled PDF reports from saved dashboards. If Wazuh exposes that feature, MSSPs can build the client report themselves, and the willingness to pay for it shrinks. ([evidence](https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/reporting/report-dashboard-index/; concept:mvp.features[2]))

### competitor-analyst — 5/10, conditional

The repositioning is sound. It builds on sysmon-modular and Yamato, drops Sentinel and Cribl-style volume claims, and targets MSSPs who keep their own Wazuh. But the new paid layer overlaps Wazuh's own features: CIS SCA policies already check auditpol subcategories per agent, agent.conf groups push per-tenant collection, and Wazuh 5.0 adds scheduled, emailed reports. Wazuh is now the most dangerous competitor.

Strongest point: No vendor sells replay-tested, maintained Windows coverage packs for Wazuh that handle both Sysmon flavours and include a migration path. That is a real, narrow niche that Wazuh itself has not filled.

- **major**: The coverage verifier and drift alerts largely duplicate Wazuh Security Configuration Assessment (SCA). Wazuh ships CIS SCA policies that already run auditpol checks per agent, for example 'Audit Logon' Success and Failure, 'Audit Process Creation' and 'Audit Security Group Management'. Custom SCA YAML can check registry values (log sizes), running processes (Sysmon) and command output, and results show in the dashboard and alert when a check changes state. Per-tenant rollout of eventchannel collection and SCA policies is native through agent groups and agent.conf. An MSSP engineer can rebuild most of the 'stateful layer' with free features and no extra agent-side script, and Wazuh could ship a 'Windows logging coverage' SCA policy in a point release. ([evidence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wazuh/wazuh/v4.9.0/ruleset/sca/windows/cis_win2022.yml ; https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/capabilities/sec-config-assessment/index.html ; https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/capabilities/sec-config-assessment/creating-custom-policies.html ; https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/reference/centralized-configuration.html ; concept:mvp.features[1]))
- **major**: The concept says 'free Wazuh has no scheduled per-client evidence report', but the Wazuh 5.0 Reporting module supports scheduled and on-demand reports from saved dashboards, visualizations and searches, delivered by email under indexer RBAC. With a saved per-agent-group dashboard, a free monthly client PDF is a configuration task. The report runner's uniqueness shrinks to report content, such as logon and RDP narratives and coverage status, rather than the ability to produce and schedule a report. ([evidence](https://documentation.wazuh.com/5.0-beta/user-manual/wazuh-indexer/modules/reporting.html ; concept:mvp.features[2].why))
- **major**: Huntress remains the most dangerous channel competitor, with Wazuh close behind. Narrowing to MSSPs that 'keep their own Wazuh stack' avoids competing on volume, but it targets the segment that managed SIEMs bundling a SOC (Huntress, Blumira, LimaCharlie MSSP tiers) are pulling away. The addressable base shrinks while the product is being built, and the concept already calls it lifestyle-scale. — _concept:risks[2] ; concept:buyer.company_profile ; concept:competitors[4] ; round-1 sources (huntress.com smart-filtering blog, limacharlie.io/pricing)_
- **minor**: Low-cost Wazuh MSSP add-ons and open-source MSSP layers set the price anchor. Search results show one-off listings for a 'Wazuh Multi-Tenant Client Dashboard' at $349 and monthly Wazuh report templates at $34.99; I could not open these listings to verify them. SOCFortress CoPilot (AGPL, about 550 stars) offers an open-source MSSP console on Wazuh and Graylog with a tenant-scoped customer portal. Against these anchors, $9 per tenant per month ($450/month at 50 tenants) is a hard sell unless replay-tested coverage proof is clearly better. ([evidence](https://github.com/socfortress/CoPilot ; concept:pricing.entry_price))
- **minor**: SOC Prime and similar content vendors could still add collection guidance, and SOCFortress publishes free Wazuh rulesets for Windows and Sysmon. The revision did not address this, and the content layer stays crowded. — _concept:revision_log (SOC Prime entry: not changed)_
- **minor**: For the logon and RDP evidence report, ManageEngine ADAudit Plus (from $245/yr) and Netwrix still offer turnkey reports. MSSPs whose clients mainly want an access-review PDF may resell those tools instead of Wazuh-derived reports. — _concept:pricing.comparables[0]_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: Free maintained configs (sysmon-modular, Yamato) already cover the core product — Repositioned to build on and credit them. Paid value moved to rollout, drift, replay proof and reports, but that layer now overlaps Wazuh SCA and reporting (new objection 1).
- ✅ resolved: Sentinel bundles curated Windows event sets and DCR filtering — Sentinel dropped from v1 and added only on paid demand.
- ✅ resolved: Cribl is free to 1TB/day with Windows reduction packs — The pitch no longer rests on volume reduction, and Cribl is named as a free competitor.
- ❌ not resolved: Flat or per-sensor MSP SIEMs (Huntress, Blumira, LimaCharlie) remove the volume ROI; Huntress is the most dangerous competitor — The volume argument is gone, but narrowing to DIY-Wazuh MSSPs puts the product in the segment these platforms are absorbing. The channel threat remains.
- ❌ not resolved: SOC Prime and content vendors could add collection recommendations — Not changed, and minor. The content layer stays crowded (SOCFortress free Wazuh rules).
- ❌ not resolved: Logon pack competes with ADAudit Plus — Price corrected and competitor kept. Positioning on the MSSP's Wazuh is reasonable, but the turnkey alternative remains.

Would change my mind: In 15 interviews, at least 6 Wazuh MSSPs say they know SCA and Wazuh reporting but still would not build per-tenant coverage checks and client reports themselves; A side-by-side showing QuietWin catches drift (stopped Sysmon, overwritten GPO, shrunk logs) and IR-question gaps that stock CIS SCA policies miss on the same lab hosts; Wazuh Inc. agreeing to list or partner on the packs, which turns the most likely copier into a channel; Pilot renewals at month 3 showing that the monthly report and drift monitoring are valued beyond the one-off deployment

### security-practitioner — 6/10, conditional

The revision fixes most round-1 problems. It narrows to Wazuh, supports both Sysmon flavours, makes 'Keep' the default and drops the canaries. Two practitioner gaps remain. Wazuh archives cannot recover events that the agent dropped, so the 'Lean' safety net is false. The RMM verifier also duplicates Wazuh-native SCA and group config, which an MSSP already trusts. A coverage verdict does not prove the events arrived.

Strongest point: The product runs locally with no hosted logs. 'Keep' is the default, the replay lab tests IR questions, and the client report gives the MSSP a concrete deliverable it can trust and resell.

- **major**: The 'Lean' profile drops events in Wazuh agent eventchannel XPath queries, and the concept names Wazuh compressed archives as the full-fidelity safety net. Wazuh archives only hold events that the manager received, so events filtered at the agent never reach archives.json. The safety net does not cover the drops it is meant to back. An IR lead who reads the docs will catch this, and the drops stay unrecoverable. Real options: keep full collection and filter with manager-side rules (archive everything, alert on less), or keep a local full-size Windows log retention window on each host. ([evidence](https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/manager/event-logging.html; concept:mvp.features (Two profiles per role)))
- **major**: The paid 'stateful layer' is a custom verifier pushed through RMM, but Wazuh already does this natively. SCA policies run scheduled per-agent command and registry checks (auditpol output, Sysmon service and config hash, EventLog MaxSize) and report pass/fail changes into Wazuh. Agent groups (agent.conf) push localfile/eventchannel config per tenant. A practitioner would rather run vendor-native SCA YAML than grant a solo vendor's signed scripts execution on 50 tenants through RMM. Rebuild the verifier as SCA policies plus group config, and keep RMM only for GPO and Sysmon install. That cuts both the trust burden and the 'Wazuh could add it' gap to a thin layer. ([evidence](https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/capabilities/sec-config-assessment/creating-custom-policies.html; https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/reference/centralized-configuration.html; concept:mvp.features (Coverage verifier run per host via RMM)))
- **major**: Configuration state is not delivery. Correct auditpol and Sysmon settings still lose events when the agent's client buffer overflows, when EPS limits are hit, when channels are disabled, or when an agent goes silent. A monthly client report that says 'covered' on config checks alone gives false assurance, and that report is a liability for the MSSP. Drift checks need an end-to-end heartbeat: generate a known test event per channel on each host and alert when it does not land in the indexer. — _concept:mvp.features (Wazuh decoders, rules and dashboards ... coverage status)_
- **minor**: Staged rings, signed packs, auditpol preview and per-tenant rollback appear only under hardest_parts, not as MVP features. Overriding a client's existing advanced audit subcategories through GPO can still collide with EDR-required or legacy-category settings, for example when 'Force audit policy subcategory settings' is off. Rollback needs a captured per-host auditpol backup taken before any change. — _concept:build.hardest_parts_
- **minor**: Most small MSSPs run one shared Wazuh manager and indexer for all their clients. Per-client reports and dashboards depend on strict agent-group and label hygiene, and a report runner that holds indexer credentials able to read every tenant is a cross-client leakage risk if it is mislabelled. Each report query needs a scope limited to one tenant.

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: The why-now rests on built-in Sysmon, which covers few SMB servers; packs must support both flavours, migration and XML-field parsing. — Packs now support standalone and built-in Sysmon with migration and XML-field parsing; why_now is reframed around migrating across the two flavours.
- ❌ not resolved: Source-side drops are unrecoverable forensic gaps, and there is no archive safety net. — 'Keep' as the default and the IR checklist help. But the named safety net, Wazuh archives, does not hold events dropped by agent-side XPath, so 'Lean' drops are still unrecoverable.
- ✅ resolved: Free Sentinel event sets and Wazuh defaults may already cover the need; the paid delta is unproven. — Sentinel is out of v1. For Wazuh, the delta is now audit policy, Sysmon, drift and reports rather than filtering. Pilots still have to measure it, and part of the drift value overlaps with native SCA.
- ✅ resolved: The ransomware-precursor pack duplicates EDR, and the canaries are slow and noisy. — Canary folders and active response are removed; only the mapped Sigma detections remain as a baseline.
- ❌ not resolved: RMM push to many tenants is a change-management and supply-chain risk, and needs signing, staged rings and rollback. — These are acknowledged in hardest_parts but are not MVP features. RMM execution scope actually grew with the verifier, where Wazuh-native SCA would avoid it.
- ✅ resolved: Logon evidence needs high-volume events (4624/4634/4778/4779), which conflicts with the volume-cut promise on RDS hosts. — The RDS 'Keep' profile keeps these events, and volume reduction is no longer promised or pitched.

Would change my mind: The 'Lean' profile is redesigned so that every drop can be recovered (manager-side filtering with archives on, or a documented local retention window), and an IR tabletop at a pilot answers lateral-movement and logon-timeline questions.; The verifier ships as Wazuh SCA policies plus agent-group config, with RMM used only for GPO and Sysmon install, and 3 pilot MSSPs accept it after a security review.; An end-to-end per-channel heartbeat test event goes into the report, and at least one pilot shows it catching a real delivery gap (buffer drop, silent agent) that the config checks missed.; A pilot rollout to 20 or more tenants with a pre-change auditpol backup, staged rings and at least one tested rollback, with no EDR or audit conflicts.

### technical-feasibility — 6/10, conditional

The revision cut the impossible parts. Wazuh only, no simulator, no canaries and a local report runner make this buildable. Three gaps remain. The 3-month plan still runs over once Wazuh learning and month-one onboarding are counted. The report and volume features assume data that Wazuh does not index by default. And the replay lab has no defined way to evaluate Sigma rules.

Strongest point: Scope is now one SIEM and Windows only, squarely in the founder's specialty, with no hosted service. Wazuh's eventchannel XML fields handle localized built-in Sysmon, so the hard parts are known and bounded.

- **major**: Schedule is still optimistic. Thirteen weeks have to cover: 3-5 weeks learning Wazuh (the concept's own estimate); 4 role packs x Keep/Lean x standalone/built-in Sysmon; a verifier; decoders, rules and dashboards; a PDF report runner; signed RMM rollout with rings and rollback; compliance and DPIA templates; and a replay lab of ~20 techniques per role (~80 tests). Paid onboarding also starts in month one. A realistic figure is 4-5 months, or 3 months with the lab and Lean profiles deferred. — _concept:build.months_to_mvp; concept:build.hardest_parts; concept:mvp.features_
- **major**: Wazuh indexes alerts, not events. Archives are off by default and need logall_json plus extra index storage before raw events can be searched. Per-event-ID volume and a complete logon/RDP/logoff report therefore only work in one of two ways. Either the packs ship rules that raise every 4624/4634/4778/4779/4672 event to an indexed alert level, which adds the indexer volume the product is meant to tame, or the MSSP indexes archives. Wazuh Cloud has no shell on the manager, so the runner can only use APIs. The concept doesn't choose between these designs. ([evidence](https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/manager/event-logging.html; concept:mvp.features))
- **minor**: 'Publishing which Sigma/Wazuh rules still fire' has no defined mechanism. There is no maintained pySigma Wazuh backend: PyPI has only an OpenSearch backend, which needs custom field mappings for win.eventdata.*. Converting Sigma rules to Wazuh XML rules is a project in its own right. A cheaper route is to run Sigma over the lab's EVTX exports with an existing engine such as Hayabusa or Chainsaw, and to test only the Wazuh rules the packs ship. ([evidence](https://pypi.org/pypi/pySigma-backend-opensearch/json))
- **minor**: Two pieces could be reused but are not. Wazuh agent groups already push localfile/XPath config per tenant. Wazuh SCA policies, including the shipped CIS Windows checks, already assess audit-policy settings and report pass/fail into Wazuh. Building an RMM-run verifier and an RMM config push duplicates both. Note that SCA command checks pushed from the manager need sca.remote_commands enabled on agents. That is a security trade-off, unless the policy is placed locally by RMM. ([evidence](https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/reference/centralized-configuration.html; https://documentation.wazuh.com/current/user-manual/capabilities/sec-config-assessment/how-to-configure.html))
- **minor**: Safe rollout (signed packs, staged rings, per-tenant rollback) depends on what each RMM supports: NinjaOne, Datto, N-able, ConnectWise and others differ. Audit policy also has two delivery paths: domain GPO, and local auditpol for workgroup servers. Name one RMM and both paths for v1, or rollout safety becomes per-customer custom work.

- ✅ resolved: Coverage-versus-volume simulator across SIEMs is the hardest feature — Simulator removed. It is replaced by Wazuh-only volume reporting, which raises a narrower data-availability issue (alerts versus archives).
- ✅ resolved: Built-in Sysmon covers a minority and can't coexist with standalone — Packs support both flavours with migration. Wazuh collects eventchannel XML fields, which are not localized.
- ✅ resolved: Maintenance load underestimated — One SIEM shrinks the matrix. The lab is still listed as a hardest part and should be automated from day one.
