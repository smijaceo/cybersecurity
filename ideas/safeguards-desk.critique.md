## Round 2

### market-skeptic — 5/10, conditional

Resegmenting to firms with 5,000+ consumers ties the paid tier to a real 314.4(d)(2) duty. But the segment is still uncounted. No buyer evidence has been added. The duty can be met by 'continuous monitoring' that MDR bundles already claim. Insurers rarely demand pen tests from small firms, and enforcement is weak. Demand is plausible but unproven.

Strongest point: For firms above 5,000 consumers, annual pen testing and six-monthly scans are a concrete, dated legal duty that WISP sellers don't deliver, and the price sits far below standalone pen tests.

- **major**: The new wedge rests on a segment nobody has sized. We don't know how many 3-20 person tax, CPA and mortgage offices hold data on 5,000+ consumers, or whether they know it. Sources disagree on how to count consumers (active versus all retained records), so many owners will self-classify under the line and buy the $39 Core tier or the free IRS template. The concept lists this as its first open question. Without a count, the 40-client target can't be judged against the size of the market. — _concept:open_questions[0] ; concept:buyer.company_profile ; https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=314&section=314.6_
- **major**: Under 314.4(d)(2), the annual pen test and six-monthly scans are needed only 'absent effective continuous monitoring'. SBTPG/Watch Cloud already sells MDR plus 'Microsoft 365 & Google Workspace monitoring' through the tax-pro channel, and MSPs will say their RMM/EDR stack is continuous monitoring. Many firms above 5,000 consumers will reasonably believe they already meet the testing duty, which shrinks the market that is legally forced to buy. The concept's own continuous M365 monitor also blurs the line. ([evidence](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=314&section=314.4 ; https://www.sbtpg.com/all-in-one-cybersecurity/ ; concept:go_to_market.wedge))
- **major**: The demand evidence still does not come from the named buyer. problem.evidence is regulation text plus old HN, ServerFault and StackExchange posts from tech-adjacent small firms. No tax-office, CPA or mortgage-broker owner or MSP asks for verification or testing. The revision log admits the problem was not changed. The pain is mandated on paper, but there is no evidence it is felt or budgeted. — _concept:problem.evidence ; concept:revision_log[-1] ; concept:risks[1]_
- **major**: The levers that turn a duty into budget are weak for this segment. FTC enforcement against sub-20-person firms is rare. Industry guides to cyber-insurance requirements (seen only in search results, not opened) say small-firm applications gate on MFA, EDR and backups, and treat pen-test or scan summaries as optional evidence asked for at higher limits. Owners already pay $999 for a WISP plus $297-$697 for insurance, and have little outside pressure to add about $2,150 a year. — _concept:pricing.rationale ; concept:open_questions[1] ; concept:risks[1]_
- **minor**: The mortgage-broker segment is now the stated all-year anchor, but there are still no need statements or counts for it. Older CSBS figures (from search results) suggest state-licensed mortgage companies number in the low tens of thousands, with most having 1-5 originators, so many may fall below the threshold. The concept also does not say how they relate to the state-regulator exams that may already cover Safeguards. — _concept:go_to_market.first_customers_
- **minor**: The partner-first channel assumes MSPs want a third party grading their tenants. Nothing in the input shows MSP demand for it. — _concept:risks[3]_

- ✅ resolved: The FTC 30-day clock wedge rarely fires (under 1% of offices a year, 500+ consumer threshold). — The clock is demoted to a free first-hour card. The wedge is now the recurring (d)(2) testing duty.
- ❌ not resolved: No demand evidence from the named buyer (tax, bookkeeping or mortgage owners). — Not changed. The concept moves it to risks and the riskiest-assumption test, which is the right test, but it has not been run.

### competitor-analyst — 5/10, conditional

The pivot to M365 checks plus 314.4(d)(2) testing does sidestep the tax-vertical WISP and MDR bundlers. It also puts the product in front of the MSP tool stack: Kaseya vPenTest, ConnectSecure scans, free M365 baselines (Lighthouse, CIPP, Maester) and Cynomi's FTC Safeguards vCISO platform. Those are the MSP partners' own tools. Vertical WISP SaaS such as Kompflow at $99/yr also undercuts the Core tier.

Strongest point: A human, credentialed pen test with a dated 314.4(d)(2) report, scoped by the 314.6 threshold, is something tax-vertical WISP and bundle vendors don't deliver today.

- **major**: The most dangerous competitor is now Kaseya, with the MSP stack around it. The concept's channel is MSPs 'that lack security-testing staff', but those MSPs don't need staff: Kaseya's vPenTest runs automated internal and external pen tests monthly or on demand and is pitched as producing compliance-ready reports. ConnectSecure sells scheduled vulnerability scanning plus compliance reporting to MSPs 'in highly regulated industries'. An MSP that owns these tools can deliver 314.4(d)(2) evidence itself and keep the margin. It has no reason to resell a solo founder's manual test at 30% off. Kaseya also owns Compliance Manager GRC, so a Safeguards template on top is a short step. ([evidence](https://www.kaseya.com/products/network-penetration-testing-tool/))
- **major**: The read-only M365 posture monitor, the concept's core feature, is commodity in the MSP channel. Microsoft 365 Lighthouse gives CSP partners SMB security baselines and multi-tenant risk views at no extra cost. CIPP (open source, built for MSPs) does multi-tenant standards and compliance reporting. Maester runs 2,000+ read-only checks against CIS/CISA baselines for free. The MFA, leaver, forwarding-rule and admin-role checks in the MVP are a subset of what partners already run. What's left to differentiate is the mapping to Safeguards sections and the report wording, not the detection. ([evidence](https://maester.dev/))
- **major**: Cynomi already sells an FTC Safeguards vCISO platform to MSPs and MSSPs. It automates risk assessments, program documentation and compliance reporting, and names mortgage brokers, tax preparers and accounting firms as targets. That is the partner console, tailored-WISP and risk-assessment layer of this MVP. It is already funded and already in the MSP channel. With Cynomi plus vPenTest, an MSP gets the whole Program tier from existing vendors. ([evidence](https://cynomi.com/?p=12890))
- **major**: Vertical WISP SaaS has moved toward 'continuous'. SafeGuardGRC/Kompflow sells a firm-specific WISP aligned with FTC 314 and Pub 4557 for $499 the first year, then $99/year, with auto-updates. Its higher tiers advertise a risk assessment, IR playbooks, vendor inventory, insurer questionnaires and 'continuous control testing'. The Core tier at $39/month (~$470/yr) is about 5x that price, and a WISP 'regenerated from findings' will not stay unique. ([evidence](https://www.safeguardgrc.com/))
- **minor**: The competitor table still leaves out local IT/security consultancies and managed-IT hosts aimed at accountants (Verito, Rightworks, ACE), which sell vulnerability scans and pen tests as add-ons. A credentialed human tester is still the only clear edge, and it doesn't scale beyond about 40-60 clients.
- **minor**: The SBTPG/Watch Cloud bundle remains a risk. A tier with an external scan or an M365 check would fit its per-device model, and it is paid from refund-transfer fees with no cash outlay. The concept accepts this risk as relationship-based with no product moat.

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: SBTPG/Watch Cloud bundles the WISP, incident team and MDR through the bank-product channel and was not in the table. — Now the top competitor. The concept repositions as a verification and testing layer beside endpoint bundles rather than a rival program. The risk that it adds a scan tier remains, as a minor objection.
- ✅ resolved: Verito's $999 recurring WISP was missed and pricing was framed against SOC 2 platforms. — Verito and ACE are now in the table and pricing is set openly against them. The listed comparables are still SOC 2/KnowBe4 ceilings rather than vertical or MSP comparables.
- ✅ resolved: The FTC clock, questionnaire and tabletop differentiators were copyable within a quarter. — The clock is now a free card. The new differentiators (M365 checks, scans, tests) face a different copy threat from MSP tooling, raised as new objections.
- ✅ resolved: The insurer's breach coach and panel counsel squeeze out the paid incident allowance. — Incident hours were removed. The insurer is now the handoff target and a referral partner.
- ❌ not resolved: Unverified: SafeGuardGRC may pre-empt the MSP white-label channel. — Not in the competitor table. Now verified: Kompflow sells a $499/$99-a-year Safeguards-aligned WISP with higher tiers that claim continuous control testing.
- ✅ resolved: Free substitutes (IRS Pub 5708, tax-software WISP content) set the price anchor. — The concept concedes the free template is enough below 5,000 consumers and targets firms that owe testing. The Core tier is still exposed to free and $99 options.

Would change my mind: 2-3 MSPs that serve accounting or mortgage offices and already own vPenTest, ConnectSecure or Cynomi sign a letter of intent to resell the Program, and say what they can't get from those tools.; Evidence that insurers, examiners or clients reject automated pen-test reports for (d)(2) and want a human-led test, so a manual test becomes a requirement rather than a preference.; A price comparison for a 10-user office showing that vPenTest plus Cynomi plus MSP labour costs well over $2,150/yr, or that MSPs won't take on Safeguards scope.; Five owners of tax or mortgage offices above 5,000 consumers who already use Kompflow, Verito or an MSP still pay the $490 testing check.

### security-practitioner — 6/10, conditional

The revision makes real security work the core: read-only M365 checks, external scans and an annual test replace owner self-attestation and the risky incident engine. But a monthly findings email is too slow to catch BEC. Pen-test scope skips workstations and remote access, where tax offices get breached. A multi-tenant app with standing read access to many financial firms concentrates risk.

Strongest point: Founder-delivered six-monthly scans and an annual test, combined with read-only M365 checks, give small offices real evidence of configuration and the (d)(2) deliverable that WISP vendors never provide.

- **major**: The monitor reports by monthly email, but the signals it collects are attacks in progress. An external forwarding rule, a new Global Admin or a risky sign-in means business email compromise is happening now. Learning about it up to 30 days later is post-mortem evidence, not detection. On Entra ID Free tenants (Business Basic/Standard), sign-in and audit logs are kept for only 7 days, so a monthly pull misses most activity unless the product polls daily and stores the data. It needs near-real-time alerts to the owner and MSP for high-severity changes, and daily collection, or it does not reduce BEC risk or meaningfully address 314.4(c)(8) user-activity monitoring. — _concept:mvp.features[1] ('monthly findings email'); https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/monitoring-health/reference-reports-data-retention (Free: sign-ins and audit logs 7 days)_
- **major**: The penetration test covers only 'internet-facing services and the M365 tenant', within a budget of about 8 founder hours per client per year that must also cover two scans, a tabletop and the report. Small tax and mortgage offices usually expose almost nothing externally. Their real compromise paths are phishing, infostealers or remote-access tools on workstations, shared local admin accounts, and tax or LOS software on office PCs. A test that never touches endpoints or the internal network produces a clean dated report while the main attack surface goes untested. That is checkbox compliance, the problem round 1 flagged in a new form, and an incident on an untested workstation would expose it. — _concept:mvp.features[2] (scope: internet-facing services and M365 tenant); concept:build.hardest_parts[2] (~8 hours/year per Program client); https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=314&section=314.4 ((d)(2)(i) testing 'of your information systems ... based on relevant identified risks')_
- **major**: The checks the concept lists need tenant-wide application permissions: AuditLog.Read.All for sign-ins, MailboxSettings.Read for inbox rules on every mailbox, Policy/Directory reads, and app-only Exchange Online access for forwarding. These permissions stay granted indefinitely across dozens of financial firms' tenants. One stolen app secret or certificate, or a compromise of the founder's laptop, becomes a read path into every client and a ready map of their weaknesses (the findings database). Attackers have abused OAuth apps this way before. A solo founder running the platform with no 24/7 coverage is a concentrated supply-chain risk. MSPs and the 314.4(f) oversight duty will ask for evidence the concept does not yet plan: certificate auth held in an HSM or Key Vault, per-tenant app registrations or GDAP, Conditional Access for workload identities, an independent assessment, and a breach notification commitment. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[0]; concept:risks[4]; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/mailfolder-list-messagerules?view=graph-rest-1.0 (Application: MailboxSettings.Read); https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/signin-list?view=graph-rest-1.0 (Application: AuditLog.Read.All)_
- **minor**: Risk detections need Entra ID P1 or P2, and Business Basic/Standard tenants have neither. Risky sign-ins are kept for only 7 days on Free. On the cheaper tenants many of these offices run, 'risky sign-ins' will mostly be empty. The monitor then shrinks to configuration checks (MFA, admins, forwarding, stale accounts). The product should say so per tenant, and perhaps recommend Business Premium as a finding. ([evidence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/riskdetection-list?view=graph-rest-1.0 (P1 or P2 required)))
- **minor**: The configuration checks repeat what MSPs already run for free. Maester, CISA ScubaGear, Microsoft Secure Score and Lighthouse cover MFA, admin roles and forwarding, Maester with thousands of read-only checks mapped to CIS/CISA that never leave the tenant. A competent MSP will see the monitor as a lighter version of tools it has and value only the Safeguards mapping, the testing and the reports. A third-party vendor's findings may also conflict with these tools' results. ([evidence](https://maester.dev/))

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: The program was mostly self-attested paperwork with no technical verification of M365, Google or endpoints. — A read-only Graph/Exchange monitor for MFA, leavers, forwarding, admin roles and risky sign-ins is now the core feature. Its monthly cadence and licence limits remain new issues.
- ✅ resolved: The program omitted the 314.4(c)(8) monitoring/logging and (d)(2) testing controls. — The six-monthly external scans and annual test meet (d)(2) on paper. (c)(8) is only partly covered: a monthly pull of 7-day logs is not user-activity monitoring, and the test scope is narrow (see new objections).
- ✅ resolved: The incident clock asked owners forensic questions that decide reportability and could produce a confidently wrong 'not reportable' record. — Reportability reasoning was removed. The card presumes reportability, routes to the insurer or counsel and records facts only.
- ✅ resolved: Insurer breach counsel would sideline a non-privileged third-party incident timeline. — The record is now facts-only handoff material to the breach coach. The insurer hotline comes first.
- ✅ resolved: Owner-run PowerShell hardening risks lockouts, makes the vendor a privileged third party, and the vendor's own security posture was missing. — Scripts were removed and changes need separate written authorization. Vendor security is now listed as a hardest part, but standing multi-tenant read access raises a larger concentration risk (new objection).
- ✅ resolved: The marketing overstated duties for firms under 5,000 consumers that 314.6 exempts. — Scoping at onboarding, resegmentation to 5,000+ consumers, and a Core tier labelled as good practice.

Would change my mind: The monitor collects data daily and sends same-day alerts for external forwarding, new privileged roles and MFA removal; a 10-tenant pilot detects and contains at least one real or simulated BEC indicator within 24 hours.; The pen-test scope includes an authenticated internal and endpoint review (local admins, remote-access tools, EDR presence, patch levels) plus a phishing-resilience check, and a time study shows it fits the hours budget or the price is raised to cover it.; A documented vendor security design (certificate credentials in an HSM or Key Vault, per-tenant isolation or GDAP, least-privilege scopes listed, workload-identity Conditional Access, an external review) that two MSPs accept in their vendor assessment.; MSPs already running Maester or ScubaGear say in pilots that the Safeguards mapping and testing are worth reselling on top.

### technical-feasibility — 7/10, conditional

The revision builds on the founder's real skills: a read-only M365 monitor, external scans and a manual pen test. Three months is plausible for the monitor, WISP generator and reports. Remaining risks: the app becomes a credential vault into many financial tenants, Exchange checks need a directory role in each tenant, signals depend on licence, the partner console inflates scope, and the ~8 h/client budget understates testing.

Strongest point: The scope is now small and squarely within the founder's skills: read-only Graph checks, external scanning and manual testing, with no reportability engine, legal tables or client-side scripts.

- **major**: Holding admin-consented application permissions (AuditLog.Read.All, Directory/Policy read, MailboxSettings read, Exchange.ManageAsApp) for dozens of financial-firm tenants makes one app secret or certificate a key to all of them. Read-only scopes still expose inbox rules, sign-in logs and the full user directory, which is exactly the reconnaissance a BEC attacker wants. The revision names vendor security as a hardest part but does not design it: certificate-only credentials in an HSM or Key Vault, per-tenant token isolation, no standing human access, and a 314.4(f)-ready security program. A solo founder has to build this to production quality inside the same three months. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[0], concept:mvp.features[1], https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/authenticationmethodsroot-list-userregistrationdetails?view=graph-rest-1.0_
- **major**: Graph does not expose external forwarding settings (mailbox ForwardingSmtpAddress, remote-domain auto-forward, outbound spam policy). These need Exchange Online PowerShell app-only access, which requires Exchange.ManageAsApp plus a Microsoft Entra directory role assigned to the app's service principal in each client tenant, with certificate authentication and a PowerShell runtime in the backend. That is a per-tenant onboarding step beyond one-click admin consent, and small-office owners or wary MSPs may refuse it. It also complicates a Python/Graph-only backend. ([evidence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/exchange/app-only-auth-powershell-v2?view=exchange-ps ; concept:mvp.features[1]))
- **major**: Signal coverage varies by licence. Risky users and risk detections need Entra ID P2, which few 3-20-seat offices have. Sign-in log and registration-detail reports are tied to premium tiers, so they may be missing on Business Basic or Standard tenants. MFA coverage then has to fall back to per-user authentication-method enumeration and a check on security defaults or conditional access. The concept names this risk, but the MVP needs a per-SKU check matrix and test tenants for each SKU before launch, or the monthly evidence quietly varies by client. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[1], https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/signin-list?view=graph-rest-1.0 ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/riskyuser-list?view=graph-rest-1.0_
- **major**: Founder hours are underestimated. An annual pen test of external services plus an M365 tenant (scoping, written authorization, testing, retest, report), two scans with triage, a tabletop and an annual report will not fit in about 8 hours per client unless the test is a templated tool run. A templated run risks not counting as 'penetration testing' under 314.4(d)(2). At a realistic 15-25 hours, 40 clients needs 600-1,000 hours a year beside running the platform. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[2], concept:pricing.entry_price, concept:build.team_
- **minor**: A white-label multi-client MSP partner console in the v1 feature list adds multi-level tenancy (MSP > client > users), role-based access, branding and report theming. That is scope creep for a three-month solo MVP. Defer it, and serve the first MSP partners with exported PDFs. — _concept:mvp.features[5], concept:build.months_to_mvp_
- **minor**: Multi-tenant apps from unverified publishers face consent warnings and blocks. Microsoft publisher verification (a partner program ID and a verified domain) and a stable consent flow should be done before outreach, or the first admin consents will stall.

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: Program omitted 314.4(d)(2) scanning/testing and the 5,000-consumer branching — Six-monthly scans, annual pen test and onboarding scoping by the 314.6 threshold now in MVP and pricing tiers; delivery hours remain underestimated (new objection).
- ✅ resolved: Lawyer-reviewed reportability tree and 50-state table outside the one-month plan — Both removed from scope; the first-hour card hands off to counsel or the insurer, which takes the content burden off the critical path.
- ❌ not resolved: App holds sensitive client data and needs its own security program; out-of-band incident access — Data held is narrower (no SSNs, returns or mail content) and months_to_mvp went from 1 to 3, but the app now holds standing read access to many tenants. The security design is still only named as a hard part. Out-of-band access matters less now that the incident card is static.
- ✅ resolved: No API for FTC submission or desktop tax software — FTC pre-fill dropped; inventory now comes mainly from M365 Graph rather than tax software.
- ✅ resolved: PowerShell hardening scripts risk lockouts across mixed configurations — Scripts removed; fixes go to the firm's IT person or MSP, and founder changes need a separate written authorization.
- ✅ resolved: Bursty incident support overwhelms a solo founder — No incident hours are sold; the card hands off to the insurer and counsel. The burst risk has moved to testing season (see the hours objection).

Would change my mind: A working prototype that onboards 3 real tenants (Business Basic, Standard and Premium) with one consent flow and returns MFA, forwarding and admin-role findings on each, with the Exchange role step timed and accepted; A written vendor security design (certificate-only app credentials in a managed key store, per-tenant isolation, audit log, MFA, no standing access) reviewed by an outside tester before the first client connects; Time logs from 3 pilot pen tests plus scans showing 10 h or less per client, with a report format that a lawyer or insurer accepts as 314.4(d)(2) evidence; The partner console moved to post-MVP, and the MVP feature list confirmed to fit in 3 months by a week-level plan

### gtm-pricing — 6/10, conditional

The revision fixes the weakest pricing: it now prices openly against Verito, sells a recurring legal deliverable (scans plus an annual test) and needs 40 clients instead of 150. But the channel that carries the plan is MSP resale. It is unproven, thinly margined and conflicted. The ARR maths ignores the 30% wholesale discount, and $2.1k/yr is double what owners now spend.

Strongest point: The wedge is now a dated, recurring legal deliverable: six-monthly scans and an annual test under 314.4(d)(2). Document vendors do not provide it, so it is a natural reason to renew.

- **major**: The go-to-market now depends on MSP resale: half of the first ten customers and the core channel. But the concept's own risks say MSPs may treat findings about their clients' tenants as a threat. - **major**: The founder would have to learn a new domain. Their Windows/M365 skills cover the hardening checklist and the technical review. But the product's value claims rest on GLBA/Safeguards interpretation, 50 state breach-notification laws, reportability decisions and possibly acting as an outsourced Qualified Individual. Learning enough to be credible and safe would take about 2-3 months, plus paying for lawyer review of the decision tree. Note that 16 CFR 314.6 exempts firms with fewer than 5,000 consumers, which covers most 1-10 person offices, from the written risk assessment, written incident response plan and testing duties. A founder who doesn't know the rule well would oversell the very features being charged for.
