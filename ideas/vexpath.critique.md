## Round 2

### market-skeptic — 5/10, conditional

The revision is more honest and cheaper, but demand is still unproven. The new Art. 14(8) hook covers only actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents, not routine not_affected triage, so the paid driver is downstream OEM requests nobody has evidenced. Narrowing to Yocto makers running Dependency-Track also shrinks an already unsized market.

Strongest point: Binding CRA dates plus a cheap, self-hosted, DT-native add-on aimed at embedded makers the enterprise SBOM vendors ignore; the test now asks for paid LOIs and a named approver.

- **major**: The repositioned compliance hook does not carry the product. Art. 14(8) requires informing users, 'where appropriate' in machine-readable form, only after the manufacturer becomes aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident. That is a rare event for a mid-size maker and is a notification duty, not a duty to publish per-release not_affected VEX across all findings. The bulk of VEXpath's value (noise cut, cross-release ledger) has no regulatory pull; it relies on OEM customer demand that the evidence does not show. ([evidence](https://prighter.com/resources/laws/cyber-resilience-act/articles/article-14; concept:why_now.summary))
- **major**: Demand evidence is unchanged: developer GitHub issues (engagement 1-43), some from open-source maintainers with no budget, and none from a Yocto device maker's PSIRT or CRA owner. The revision admits this in risks and moves the burden to the riskiest-assumption test, which is right, but until that test runs there is no sign of budgeted pain in the named segment. — _input:themes[theme_id=14].need_statements; concept:risks[2]; concept:revision_log (market-skeptic, 'not changed in evidence')_
- **major**: The narrowing compounds the size problem. The buyer now has to be an EU maker with 50-1,000 staff, firmware on Yocto/embedded Linux, already producing SBOMs, already running Dependency-Track or OE cve-check, and with a named CRA owner. Each filter cuts the pool; the concept itself guesses 'a few thousand' for a broader profile with no source. At EUR 3.5k ACV, even 300 customers is about EUR 1M ARR, so the segment may not support a venture-scale company. — _concept:buyer.company_profile; concept:open_questions[4]; concept:pricing.entry_price_
- **major**: Budget competition inside the CRA stack. CVD Portal sells Art. 14 reporting plus SBOM management, remediation tracking and CSAF advisories from EUR 1,188-3,588/yr, grant-eligible. VEXpath at EUR 3,480/yr roughly doubles a mid-size maker's CRA tooling spend for one sub-step that the suite already lists. Buyers who must justify spend to a regulatory or quality manager will likely accept the suite's manual VEX first. ([evidence](https://cvdportal.com/pricing; concept:pricing.rationale))
- **minor**: Urgency remains deadline-shaped: main obligations land 11 December 2027 and the revision left this unchanged. Expect thin 2027 revenue and a buying spike late in 2027, which a seed company must survive on design-partner income. — _concept:revision_log (market-skeptic, 'Urgency ... not changed')_

Round-1 objections:
- ❌ not resolved: Demand evidence is developer GitHub issues, not purchase intent from the named buyer. — Evidence unchanged; acknowledged in risks and the test now requires LOIs and a named approver, which is the right fix but not yet run.
- ❌ not resolved: Compliance hook weaker than pitched: CRA mandates neither VEX nor CSAF. — Overclaim removed, which is good, but the replacement hook (Art. 14(8)) only covers actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents, so routine triage still lacks regulatory pull.
- ❌ not resolved: Urgency is deadline-shaped and late. — Not changed by design; lower price and product-led motion reduce burn per deal but not the timing.
- ✅ resolved: WTP evidence comes from enterprise contracts and sbomify anchors prices low. — Price cut to EUR 3,480/yr and framed as an add-on beside suites; enterprise comparables kept as ceiling; WTP is now tested directly. Budget competition with suites remains (new objection).
- ✅ resolved: CSAF matching may be commoditised in Dependency-Track. — Core moved to Yocto build evidence, invalidation and signing; DT's CSAF matching becomes an input.
- ❌ not resolved: Market size for the exact profile is unquantified. — Added as an open question, but the narrowed profile makes the pool smaller and it is still unsourced.

Would change my mind: At least 4 of 15 CRA owners at 50-500-person EU Yocto device makers sign a EUR 290/month LOI with a named spend approver (the concept's own test).; At least 8 of 15 can show a written VEX or vulnerability-status request from a downstream OEM or operator in the last 12 months.; A sourced count (industry-association or Yocto/DT community data) of at least 2,000 EU makers matching the narrowed profile.; A CRA suite (CVD Portal, sbomify) agrees to resell or bundle the engine, showing a channel that reaches buyers at this ACV.; A draft harmonised standard or market surveillance guidance that expects VEX/CSAF for routine vulnerability handling.

### competitor-analyst — 4/10, no-go

The revision answers round 1: it builds on Dependency-Track, sits beside the CRA suites and names SecObserve and CRAVEX. But the new core, Yocto build evidence turned into VEX, is already covered elsewhere: by Yocto's own official sbom-cve-check, by bitvex, by VulnScout and by Lynx Vigiles. The competitor table names none of them. What is left is DT write-back, invalidation and signing, which is thin.

Strongest point: I found no tool that keeps per-release VEX decisions natively inside Dependency-Track with conservative invalidation and signing. That gives a narrow but real slot for teams that have standardised on DT.

- **fatal**: Most dangerous competitor: Bootlin's sbom-cve-check. It has been the Yocto Project's official CVE-monitoring tool since the Wrynose release, it is free (GPLv2), and it works on exactly the inputs VEXpath reads: the SPDX3 SBOM and the vex.bbclass rootfs.json. It marks kernel CVEs not applicable when the affected source file is not compiled in, which is finer than VEXpath's kernel-option rule. It also exports a Yocto VEX manifest with statements and justifications and emits not_affected for disputed CVEs. This is the 'paid additional cut' in the wedge, and every Yocto user now gets it upstream for free. ([evidence](https://bootlin.com/?p=22076 ; https://bootlin.com/blog/sbom-cve-check-updates-new-release-meta-sbom-cve-check-possible-yocto-integration-fosdem-talk/ ; concept:go_to_market.wedge ; concept:mvp.features[1]))
- **major**: Lynx (Timesys) Vigiles is an established commercial product for this exact buyer, and the concept leaves it out. Its meta-timesys Yocto layer uploads the kernel and U-Boot .config to filter CVEs and keeps a not-affected list. It markets triage decisions, release-by-release records of how each CVE was handled, VEX export, KEV prioritisation and EU CRA reporting for embedded Linux, Yocto, Buildroot and custom BSPs. It also has NXP as a BSP distribution channel. VEXpath's line that 'nobody affordable turns embedded build evidence into reviewed VEX' is not supported until it is checked against Vigiles' pricing and NXP bundle. ([evidence](https://vigiles.lynx.com/docs/build-system-docs/meta_timesys.html ; https://www.timesys.com/?p=30183 ; concept:differentiation))
- **major**: Free open-source tools already cover the rest of the embedded workbench. bitvex is a free CLI that turns Yocto SPDX into OpenVEX using kernel .config, device-tree 'disabled' peripherals and U-Boot config. It also produces delta VEX between builds and has a watch mode that re-scans when SBOMs or configs change, which is close to VEXpath's invalidation idea. VulnScout from Savoir-faire Linux imports Yocto cve-check/VEX with SPDX, has a triage UI, records VEX assessments with AI suggestions that need human approval, and exports CycloneDX and OpenVEX. Device-tree filtering already goes further than VEXpath's MVP. ([evidence](https://docs.rs/crate/bitvex/0.3.1 ; https://github.com/savoirfairelinux/vulnscout))
- **major**: What remains distinct is two-way Dependency-Track sync, invalidation of inherited decisions and signing with the customer's key. These are workflow features, not a product moat. Any of the above could add them, and so could DT 5.1, since its VEX import already exists. sbom-cve-check output plus DT VEX import already gets most teams the free importer that VEXpath plans to give away. — _concept:mvp.features[2] ; concept:mvp.features[3] ; https://dependencytrack.org/news/dependency-track-5-1_
- **minor**: The EUR 3,480/yr add-on is now priced against free upstream tooling and an incumbent embedded vendor, not only against CRA suites. A buyer adds it on top of a suite at EUR 1,188-3,588, so the triage step costs as much as the whole suite. — _concept:pricing.entry_price_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: Dependency-Track 5.1 and funded CSAF work close the gap (most dangerous competitor in round 1). — Repositioned to build on DT: it writes back into DT's analysis and consumes DT's CSAF when that lands. This conflict is gone, but DT plus free Yocto tooling still sets the free baseline.
- ✅ resolved: Missed self-serve CRA suites (CVD Portal, CRA Evidence, CRA-Ready) priced below VEXpath. — The suites are now listed, the Art. 14 workflow and evidence pack are dropped, and VEXpath is sold beside them. The add-on still costs as much as a whole suite.
- ✅ resolved: Free SecObserve and DejaCode/CRAVEX cover the VEX workbench. — Both are named and the product is differentiated on embedded build evidence. That differentiation now collides with sbom-cve-check, bitvex and VulnScout.
- ✅ resolved: sbomify is a closer rival than a partner. — The hosted portal is dropped, sbomify is framed as an integration target and a possible rival, and the risk is kept.
- ❌ not resolved: Funded enterprise vendors could add a self-serve tier within a quarter. — Unchanged by design. An embedded-specialist incumbent, Lynx Vigiles, already covers the Yocto build-evidence segment and is missing from the analysis.
- ❌ not resolved: The defensible core (CSAF matching) is a perishable feature, not a product. — The core moved to build-evidence rules, and those are already free upstream (sbom-cve-check compiled-source filtering, bitvex kernel/DT/U-Boot filters) and commercial (Vigiles kernel config filter). The feature-not-product problem remains.

Would change my mind: A side-by-side test on 3 real Yocto firmware data sets showing VEXpath resolves 30%+ more findings, with zero wrong not_affected calls, than sbom-cve-check (compiled-sources on) plus bitvex, after both are imported into DT 5.1.; Vigiles pricing or NXP-bundle terms that confirm it is out of reach or a poor fit for 50-500-person makers, plus 3 discovery calls where Vigiles or VulnScout users name a gap VEXpath fills.; A repositioning onto the free upstream tools rather than against them, for example a supported, DT-native ledger and signing layer on sbom-cve-check output, sold with Bootlin, Savoir-faire Linux or a Yocto consultancy as a signed partner.; Evidence that DT maintainers will not take Yocto VEX import and cross-release decision inheritance into scope within 12 months.

### security-practitioner — 6/10, conditional

The revision fixes most practitioner concerns: it is self-hosted, writes back to Dependency-Track, attaches evidence to every proposal, never auto-applies one and requires named sign-off. Two problems remain. Free OE tooling already filters kernel CVEs by config, and VulnScout gives Yocto teams a free triage UI, so the residual the paid engine works is smaller than pitched. The invalidation rule, as written, would also re-open most inherited decisions on every release.

Strongest point: It treats VEX as reviewed, evidence-backed decisions that stay in Dependency-Track, never auto-applies a status, and leaves data custody and the signing key with the manufacturer.

- **major**: The kernel-config evidence rule is already in free upstream tooling. OE-core's improve_kernel_cve_report.py takes the compiled-file list from the kernel SPDX and marks a version-affected CVE as 'not-applicable-config' when its programFiles were not compiled. Kernel CVEs are the bulk of embedded Linux noise. Teams on current Yocto who run this plus vex.bbclass will import an already-reduced report into DT. The 'additional cut' shown in the wedge may be small, and that is exactly the number the buyer's engineer will check. ([evidence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openembedded/openembedded-core/master/scripts/contrib/improve_kernel_cve_report.py; concept:mvp.features[1]; concept:go_to_market.wedge))
- **major**: Workflow competition from a free Yocto-native triage UI. Savoir-faire Linux's VulnScout (GPL-3.0) ingests Yocto cve-check/VEX, SPDX and CycloneDX, has a web triage UI with VEX assessments per project and variant, CI policy checks and CycloneDX/OpenVEX export. Embedded teams evaluating 'a triage tool beside our build' will find it first. VEXpath's remaining edge for a practitioner is DT write-back, cross-release invalidation, CSAF export and signing. Those are real but narrower, and the concept does not list VulnScout. ([evidence](https://github.com/savoirfairelinux/vulnscout; concept:competitors))
- **major**: The invalidation rule fights reuse. A new firmware release almost always changes some component versions and the image's build-config hash. If any change to 'build-config hash' re-opens an inherited decision, most decisions re-open every release and the cross-release time saving disappears. If the rule is scoped per component (the relevant recipe's config and patches, or the kernel options touched by the CVE's files), it must be shown to be safe. The design needs per-finding evidence fingerprints, not a global hash. As written, the rule is either unsafe or unhelpful. — _concept:mvp.features[2]; concept:build.hardest_parts[2]_
- **minor**: Two-way sync with DT analysis state is a trust risk. If analysts also edit analysis in DT, or DT 5.x applies its own VEX import or CSAF matching later, the ledger and DT can diverge. That would bring back the split source of truth the revision was meant to remove. Practitioners will need explicit precedence rules and an audit diff. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[3]_
- **minor**: Self-hosting fixes custody but shifts ops onto 1-3 person product-security teams: upgrades, offline vulnerability data, and signing-key storage (an HSM or KMS for a key whose signatures operators trust). Without Jira and CI gating in v1, decisions still have to be re-keyed into engineering workflows. — _concept:build.hardest_parts[4]; concept:mvp.out_of_scope_

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: CSAF-based noise cut misses the embedded Linux/Yocto core of the buyer base — Build evidence (CVE_STATUS, patches, kernel config) is now primary and CSAF is one input. The residual value is now challenged by free upstream kernel filtering (new objection).
- ✅ resolved: Trust in auto-suggested and inherited not_affected without reachability — Every proposal carries evidence, none is auto-applied, sign-off is named and automation is logged. The invalidation rule exists but is too coarse to be both safe and useful (new objection).
- ✅ resolved: Data custody: multi-tenant SaaS holding every release SBOM plus open vulnerabilities — Self-hosted by default, EU single-tenant hosting only for design partners, and test data stays on the prospect's premises.
- ✅ resolved: Workflow fit against Dependency-Track and a split source of truth — Decisions are written back to DT analysis and DT's CSAF matching is consumed when it ships. The remaining sync-conflict risk is a minor objection.
- ✅ resolved: KEV listing used to trigger CRA 24h/72h report drafts — KEV now only raises queue priority, and the Art. 14 workflow is left to CRA suites.
- ❌ not resolved: Thin integrations: Jira, CI gating, Yocto/SPDX 3 VEX — Yocto build-time VEX ingest was added. Jira and CI gating were deferred to v1.1.

Would change my mind: On 5 real Yocto firmware data sets, after vex.bbclass, improve_kernel_cve_report.py with SPDX compiled files, and DT 5.1, VEXpath resolves 30%+ of the remaining findings with zero wrong not_affected on engineer review.; A side-by-side with VulnScout where 3+ PSIRT leads say DT write-back, invalidation and signed CSAF justify EUR 290/month.; A per-finding invalidation design tested on two consecutive real releases: under 30% of inherited decisions re-opened and no stale not_affected surviving a relevant change.; A documented DT sync precedence model (who wins on conflict, with an audit diff) validated with a design partner.

### technical-feasibility — 7/10, conditional

Much more buildable. Self-hosting, four features and six months suit two engineers, and the riskiest rules already exist in OE-core. The remaining work is a data join: OE build evidence is keyed by recipe and CPE product, DT findings by SBOM PURL. Kirkstone BSPs lack vex.bbclass, and the kernel rule should use compiled files, not .config.

Strongest point: Rescoped MVP (no multi-tenancy, no portal, no evidence pack) fits two specialists in six months, built largely on DT 5.1, OE vex/cve-check and existing CSAF libraries.

- **major**: The core build-evidence join is harder than the feature list implies. vex.bbclass writes per-recipe status keyed by recipe name and CVE_PRODUCT (an NVD/CPE product name), with no PURLs. Dependency-Track findings are keyed by the SBOM component's PURL/CPE. Where the SBOM came from Syft/Trivy scanning a rootfs rather than Yocto's own SPDX, recipe-to-component mapping is fuzzy, and a wrong match can wrongly carry a not_affected status. v1 should require a Yocto-generated SBOM, or treat unmatched components as a hard 'no evidence' result. ([evidence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openembedded/openembedded-core/master/meta/classes/vex.bbclass))
- **major**: Coverage across Yocto releases. vex.bbclass and OE's kernel CVE enrichment script are on scarthgap but not on kirkstone. Kirkstone is still common in vendor BSPs (NXP, TI and others lag), so the ingest must also parse older cve-check JSON and handle per-branch format differences. That adds to an already tight six months and to every later release. ([evidence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openembedded/openembedded-core/kirkstone/meta/classes/vex.bbclass (404) vs scarthgap (200); concept:build.hardest_parts))
- **minor**: The kernel rule is specified as mapping kernel .config options to CVEs, but kernel CVE records name affected files, not Kconfig symbols. OE-core's improve_kernel_cve_report.py already does this properly, matching kernel CNA records against the compiled-file list from the kernel SPDX (SPDX_INCLUDE_COMPILED_SOURCES). Reuse that approach. That lowers the build risk, but it also narrows the paid 'additional cut' compared with free OE tooling. ([evidence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openembedded/openembedded-core/master/scripts/contrib/improve_kernel_cve_report.py))
- **minor**: Two-way sync with DT analysis state. DT stores one analysis per project/component/vulnerability. VEXpath's ledger keys on release, dependency path and build-config hash, and it must not overwrite analysts' edits. Conflict rules, plus the DT v4-to-v5 API differences, need tests against both majors. — _concept:build.hardest_parts_
- **minor**: A self-hosted product shifts the cost to support: upgrades, offline or air-gapped vulnerability data, and customer-key signing (HSM/PKCS#11 or file keys). This is manageable for a container next to DT, but release engineering should be budgeted from month one.

Round-1 objections:
- ✅ resolved: PURL-based CSAF matching rests on data that mostly isn't there (Siemens CSAF has no PURLs) — CSAF matching demoted to one input, limited to feeds with PURLs; build evidence is now the primary source.
- ✅ resolved: Red Hat VEX uses source-RPM PURLs that don't match scanner binary RPMs, and its format changed in 2026 — Binary-to-source-RPM mapping is now an explicit feature and hardest part, and Red Hat is no longer central for the embedded buyer. Maintenance stays a stated risk.
- ✅ resolved: Five features plus multi-tenant SaaS in 5 months for 2 engineers is too tight — Cut to 4 features, self-hosted, no portal/evidence pack/multi-tenancy, 6 months. Plausible, though the cross-release Yocto ingest still makes it tight.
- ✅ resolved: No invalidation rule for inherited not_affected decisions — Re-open on any change to version, dependency path or build-config hash; conservative and testable.
- ✅ resolved: Operating a CSAF trusted-provider portal adds ongoing operations — Portal removed; export only, and customers publish.

Would change my mind: A prototype that joins vex.bbclass/cve-check output to DT findings on 3 real firmware builds (at least one kirkstone), with match coverage reported and zero wrong carries on manual review.; Measured extra cut beyond CVE_STATUS plus OE's improve_kernel_cve_report.py of at least 20% of open findings on those builds.; Shown write-back to DT 5.1 analysis that preserves manual analyst edits across a re-import.

### gtm-pricing — 5/10, conditional

The revision makes the pricing coherent: EUR add-on beside CRA suites, an uncapped per-product unit, and a product-led channel. But a EUR 3,480 ACV sold self-hosted to a few thousand Yocto makers gives a thin revenue ceiling. Yocto 6.0 now ships free sbom-cve-check and kernel compiled-source filtering, which shrinks the paid delta buyers will pay for.

Strongest point: Coherent public EUR pricing as an add-on beside CRA suites, with an uncapped per-product-line unit that matches how CRA obligations attach and grows with portfolios.

- **major**: The free baseline moved up under the price. Yocto 6.0 made Bootlin's sbom-cve-check the official CVE tool. It consumes vex.bbclass CVE_STATUS and OpenVEX annotations and treats CVEs in sources that are not compiled in as not applicable. OE-Core's improve_kernel_cve_report.py does the same for kernel CVEs using compiled-file lists. Two of VEXpath's core evidence rules (CVE_STATUS, kernel 'code not present') are therefore free in the buyer's build. ### legal-ethics — 6/10, conditional

A defensive tool with little potential for misuse, but the hosted-only design would hold a sensitive, cross-customer map of unpatched exposures, and the concept has no data-protection plan. Its KEV-triggered 24h/72h draft misreads the CRA Article 14 trigger, and it does not settle who is liable for auto-suggested 'not_affected' calls. All of this is fixable before launch.

Strongest point: It is a defensive product with little potential for misuse. Art. 14(8) explicitly asks manufacturers to inform users in a structured, machine-readable format, and CSAF/VEX fits that request.

- **major**: Data custody is the biggest legal and ethical exposure, and the concept never addresses it. Per release, the SaaS would store each customer's full component inventory, its open 'affected' and 'under_investigation' findings, and drafts about actively exploited vulnerabilities written before disclosure. Across many makers, that is a ready-made target list for supply-chain attackers. On-premise deployment is out of scope. The plan names no hosting region, encryption or tenant-isolation design, certification (ISO 27001 or SOC 2) or breach-notification terms. DACH industrial buyers, and their own NIS2 supply-chain checks, will ask for all of these before uploading SBOMs. — _concept:mvp.out_of_scope ("On-premise deployment"); concept:mvp.features[0]; concept:mvp.features[4]; concept:build.team_
- **major**: The '24h/72h exploited-vulnerability draft triggered by a KEV listing' does not match the law. Under Art. 14(1)-(2), the trigger is the manufacturer becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability contained in its own product. Recital 68 ties this to a manufacturer establishing that a security breach affecting its users resulted from a malicious actor using the flaw. A KEV entry for an upstream component neither creates that duty nor is needed for it. Building on KEV invites two errors: over-reporting non-exploited issues to CSIRTs and ENISA, and false assurance that silence means no clock is running. The product and its marketing must describe the trigger correctly and leave the 'actively exploited' determination to the manufacturer. ([evidence](https://prighter.com/resources/laws/cyber-resilience-act/articles/article-14; https://www.eucybersecurity.org/glossary/actively-exploited-vulnerability; concept:mvp.features[4]))
- **major**: Liability for wrong 'not_affected' calls is pushed into a single risk line. Three MVP features make VEXpath a co-author of statements that customers and regulators rely on: auto-resolving findings from vendor CSAF matches, inheriting decisions across releases, and publishing signed VEX/CSAF documents. A bad PURL match or a stale inherited decision can tell downstream operators not to patch an exploitable device. The concept needs several protections before launch: human sign-off that cannot be bypassed, records showing which suggestions were automated, and an automatic re-review when a dependency path changes. Contract terms should cap liability, state that decisions belong to the customer and make clear who signs the documents (the customer's key, not VEXpath's). The founders should also budget for professional-indemnity insurance. Software is increasingly within EU product-liability rules, so contract caps alone may not shield the company. — _concept:risks[5]; concept:mvp.features[2]; concept:mvp.features[3]; concept:build.hardest_parts[1]_
- **minor**: Several phrases risk overstating what the CRA requires: 'priced for CRA compliance', 'CRA evidence pack' and VEX/CSAF as the compliance answer. Annex I Part II(1) requires a machine-readable SBOM covering at least top-level dependencies. That SBOM goes in the technical documentation and need not be public. No harmonised standard had been cited as of September 2026, and the CRA mandates neither VEX nor CSAF. The marketing must not imply conformity or a presumption of conformity. Art. 14(8)'s 'structured, machine-readable format' for informing users is the defensible hook. ([evidence](https://fossa.com/blog/sbom-requirements-cra-cyber-resilience-act; concept:one_liner; concept:open_questions[0]))
- **minor**: The hosted customer portal could publish SBOMs and 'affected' statements for releases that are still unpatched, which gives attackers a roadmap. It would also cut across the coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy the CRA requires (Art. 13(8)). Publishing should be private by default, with access controls and an embargo or release gate, so the tool can't become a disclosure channel by accident. — _concept:mvp.features[3]; https://prighter.com/resources/laws/cyber-resilience-act/articles/article-13_
- **minor**: Ingesting and redisplaying third-party vendor CSAF advisories (Red Hat, Oracle, Siemens and others) depends on each provider's terms of use and licence, including attribution and limits on redistribution or commercial reuse. The terms need checking per provider, and VEXpath should link to vendor advisories rather than republish them.

Would change my mind: A written security and data-handling design before the pilots: EU-only hosting, per-tenant encryption, private-by-default publishing, and a dated plan for SOC 2 or ISO 27001.; The exploited-vulnerability feature reframed as a manual 'mark product actively exploited' workflow with Art. 14 deadline timers, with KEV used only as a prompt to investigate, and an EU regulatory lawyer reviewing the wording.; Pilot contract and terms of service that keep decisions with the customer, cap liability, have documents signed with the customer's own key, and log every auto-suggestion and its human approver. Professional-indemnity insurance quoted at an affordable premium.; A licence check showing that the top 10 CSAF trusted providers allow commercial ingestion and matching.
